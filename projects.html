1. The Structural Problem: Modern Price Discovery is Driven by Derivatives Plumbing

Why fundamental valuation and classical price charts fail to explain intraday index velocity on the NSE

Traditional fundamental equity research assumes asset prices move exclusively when underlying corporate cash flows or macroeconomic forecasts change. Meanwhile, classical technical analysis relies on lagging geometric moving averages. Both frameworks overlook a defining structural reality of Indian capital markets: notional turnover in NSE index options routinely exceeds underlying cash equity volume by a factor of 100x to 400x.

When retail participants, proprietary desks, and institutional hedgers buy or sell index options, liquidity is heavily intermediated by automated options market makers (dealers) and high-frequency liquidity desks. Because market makers monetize the bid-ask spread and volatility risk premium rather than taking naked directional bets, their risk engines continuously enforce a delta-neutral book.

To remain delta-neutral as spot price $S$ fluctuates, dealers must mechanically buy or sell underlying index futures. The rate at which a dealer's net delta changes with respect to spot is governed by Gamma ($\Gamma = \partial \Delta / \partial S = \partial^2 V / \partial S^2$). Large clusters of Open Interest therefore lock market makers into deterministic, quantifiable futures hedging flows that either compress intraday volatility (pinning the index) or amplify directional liquidations.

POSITIVE GAMMA REGIME (NET GEX > 0) Spot > Gamma Flip ($S^*$)

Counter-Trend Dealer Hedging: Volatility Compression & Pinning

When dealers sit net long gamma, their portfolio delta moves in the same direction as the index. As NIFTY rallies, dealer delta becomes increasingly positive; to neutralize exposure, dealers mechanically sell index futures into strength. Conversely, when NIFTY drops, dealer delta turns negative, forcing them to buy index futures into weakness.

Microstructure Effect: Realized volatility compresses below implied volatility. Breakouts fail, dips are absorbed, and spot gravitates toward high-gamma strike magnets.
NEGATIVE GAMMA REGIME (NET GEX < 0) Spot < Gamma Flip ($S^*$)

Trend-Following Dealer Hedging: Volatility Expansion & Cascades

When dealers sit net short gamma (typically below heavy put walls), their portfolio delta moves inversely to the index. As NIFTY falls, dealers become increasingly long delta (`put delta` approaches $-1.0$ for customers, $+1.0$ for short dealers); to re-hedge, dealers must dump index futures into falling bids.

Microstructure Effect: Pro-cyclical hedging feedback loop. Orderbook liquidity thins out and intraday ranges expand violently into directional cascades.

2. Mathematical Formulation & Core Quantitative Pillars in Sundial

Solving 0DTE asymptotic gamma blowup, futures basis carry distortion, and non-linear Gamma Flip root-finding

Naive scripts that plug raw NSE cash spot and textbook point-gamma into Black-Scholes break down in live production during afternoon expiry sessions and across multi-expiry term structures. Sundial's quantitative core (greeks.py, gex_engine.py, and gex_history.py) is built around four production mathematical pillars:

PILLAR 01 • greeks.py :: bs_band_gamma() Eliminating 0DTE Afternoon Singularity ($\tau \to 0$)

Discrete Hedge-Band Gamma ($\Gamma_{\text{band}}$) vs. Infinitesimal Point Gamma ($\Gamma_{\text{point}}$)

Under textbook Black-Scholes, infinitesimal point gamma is $\Gamma_{\text{point}} = \frac{\phi(d_1)}{S \sigma \sqrt{T}}$. On expiry afternoon as $T \to 0$, $\sqrt{T}$ in the denominator approaches zero, causing ATM point gamma to explode toward $\infty$ and producing fictitious multi-lakh-crore GEX spikes—even though a dealer's maximum delta change across an entire strike is strictly bounded between $0$ and $1$. Sundial replaces infinitesimal point gamma with Discrete Hedge-Band Gamma ($\Gamma_{\text{band}}$), measuring the actual finite-difference change in Black-Scholes Delta across a realistic dealer rebalancing corridor of $h = 0.005$ ($\pm 0.5\%$ around spot, or a $1\%$ total underlying move):

$$\Gamma_{\text{band}}(S, K, T, r, \sigma, b) = \frac{\Delta\big(S(1+h)\big) - \Delta\big(S(1-h)\big)}{2 \cdot h \cdot S} = \frac{N(d_{1,\text{up}}) - N(d_{1,\text{down}})}{2 \cdot h \cdot S}, \quad h = 0.005$$
Where $d_{1,\text{up}} = \frac{\ln\left(\frac{S(1+h)}{K}\right) + \left(b + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2\right)T}{\sigma\sqrt{T}}$ and $d_{1,\text{down}} = \frac{\ln\left(\frac{S(1-h)}{K}\right) + \left(b + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2\right)T}{\sigma\sqrt{T}}$, enforced with a 1-hour minimum time floor ($T_{\min} = \frac{1}{365 \times 24}$). Because $|N(d_{1,\text{up}}) - N(d_{1,\text{down}})| \le 1$, $\Gamma_{\text{band}}$ is mathematically bounded on 0DTE and converges smoothly to $\Gamma_{\text{point}}$ for longer-dated tenors.
PILLAR 02 • gex_engine.py :: implied_forward() & bs_iv_from_price() Eliminating Futures Basis & Dividend Mispricing

Put-Call Parity Implied Forward ($F$) & Continuous Cost-of-Carry Rate ($b$)

Indian index options are priced off the Index Futures Forward Curve ($F$), not the cash index ($S$), because NIFTY futures trade at a variable cost-of-carry basis and factor in upcoming constituent dividends. Pricing options directly off cash $S$ with a static $r = 7\%$ systematically skews $d_1$, shifting ATM gamma and the Gamma Flip level by 40–90 points. Sundial extracts the true market-implied forward $F$ directly from the 3 nearest ATM strikes (`FORWARD_ATM_STRIKES = 3`) for each expiry using Bid-Ask Midpoints (falling back to LTP only when quotes are missing) and rejects stale outliers when $|\ln(F/S)| > 0.01$:

$$F_{\text{expiry}} = \operatorname{median}_{i \in \text{3 ATM}}\left( K_i + e^{rT}\big(C_{\text{mid}, i} - P_{\text{mid}, i}\big) \right), \qquad b_{\text{expiry}} = \frac{\ln(F_{\text{expiry}} / S)}{T}$$
Passing the continuous per-expiry carry rate $b_{\text{expiry}}$ into generalized Black-Scholes ensures $S e^{bT} = F_{\text{expiry}}$. When exchange-reported IV is zero or missing on active strikes, Sundial invokes a 50-iteration vectorized Newton-Raphson Implied Volatility solver (`bs_iv_from_price`) seeded at $\sigma_0 = 0.20$ with a $\text{Vega} > 10^{-8}$ numerical guard and clipped to $[0.01\%, 500\%]$.
PILLAR 03 • gex_engine.py & gex_history.py :: compute_gex_zscore() SEBI Circular FAOP/70616 Lot Sizes & Cross-DTE Normalization

Rupee Hedging Notional (₹ Cr / 1% Move), Scale-Free GEX Ratio & DTE-Bucketed Z-Scores

For each strike $K$ across loaded expiries, Sundial scales unit Hedge-Band Gamma into exact Indian Rupee Crores ($\text{1 Crore} = 10^7$) of futures hedging notional required per $1\%$ move in spot, configured with current SEBI/NSE contract multipliers (`NIFTY = 65`, `BANKNIFTY = 30`, `FINNIFTY = 60`, `MIDCPNIFTY = 120`, `NIFTYNXT50 = 25`, `SENSEX = 10`, `BANKEX = 15`). Because raw Net GEX naturally swells as expiration approaches, Sundial normalizes regime telemetry using both a bounded, scale-free GEX Ratio $\in [-1, +1]$ and a DTE-Bucketed 20-Session Rolling Z-Score:

$$\text{Call GEX}_K = +\frac{\Gamma_{\text{band, Call}, K} \cdot \text{OI}_{\text{Call}, K} \cdot \text{LotSize} \cdot S^2 \cdot 0.01}{10^7}, \qquad \text{Put GEX}_K = -\frac{\Gamma_{\text{band, Put}, K} \cdot \text{OI}_{\text{Put}, K} \cdot \text{LotSize} \cdot S^2 \cdot 0.01}{10^7}$$ $$\text{GEX Ratio} = \frac{\sum_K \left(\text{Call GEX}_K + \text{Put GEX}_K\right)}{\sum_K \left(|\text{Call GEX}_K| + |\text{Put GEX}_K|\right)} \in [-1, +1], \qquad Z_{\text{DTE}} = \frac{\text{Net GEX}_{\text{now}} - \mu_{\text{bucket}}}{\sigma_{\text{bucket}}}$$
Where regimes classify as POSITIVE when $\text{GEX Ratio} > +0.10$, NEGATIVE when $\text{GEX Ratio} < -0.10$, and NEUTRAL inside $[-0.10, +0.10]$. Historical snapshots in gex_history.csv are stratified into 4 time-to-expiry buckets (0DTE, 1DTE, 2-3DTE, 4+DTE) so a Thursday 0DTE reading is statistically benchmarked only against prior 0DTE sessions.
PILLAR 04 • gex_engine.py :: _vectorized_totals_across_levels() 401 × N NumPy Broadcasting (~1.5 ms)

True Non-Linear Gamma Flip Root-Finding via 2D Matrix Spot Re-Evaluation

A common flaw in basic GEX calculators is computing fixed strike GEX bars at current spot $S_0$ and simply finding where the cumulative sum of those static bars crosses zero. That approach is mathematically invalid because as spot moves from $S_0$ toward a hypothetical level $S'$, every strike's moneyness $\ln(S'/K)$, $d_1$, and $\Gamma_{\text{band}}(S')$ dynamically shifts. Sundial solves for the true dynamic Gamma Flip ($S^*$) without slow Python loops by broadcasting a 2D NumPy matrix of shape (401, N_strikes) across $[0.95 S_0, 1.05 S_0]$:

$$\mathbf{G}_{m, i} = \Gamma_{\text{band}}\big(S_m, K_i, T_i, r, \sigma_i, b_i\big) \cdot \text{OI}_i \cdot \text{LotSize} \cdot S_m^2 \cdot 10^{-9}, \qquad \text{NetGEX}(S_m) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \left(\mathbf{G}^{\text{Call}}_{m, i} - \mathbf{G}^{\text{Put}}_{m, i}\right)$$ $$S^* = S_m - \text{NetGEX}(S_m) \cdot \frac{S_{m+1} - S_m}{\text{NetGEX}(S_{m+1}) - \text{NetGEX}(S_m)}$$
Evaluates 401 hypothetical spot levels $\times$ ~140 multi-expiry rows ($\approx 56,000$ option re-pricings) in a single vectorized C-level array pass (~1.5 ms), detects all sign changes $\operatorname{sign}(\text{NetGEX}(S_m)) \neq \operatorname{sign}(\text{NetGEX}(S_{m+1}))$, selects the crossing closest to current spot, and applies exact secant linear interpolation.

3. Detailed Analysis: Second-Order GEX Modeling vs. First-Order OI Heuristics

How strike-level dollar gamma weighting extends standard Open Interest bar charts and static Max-Pain models

Conventional option chain workflows typically rely on flat contract counts (Open Interest bars) or static expiry settlement heuristics ("Max Pain"). Below is a methodological breakdown of how Sundial's second-order Greek engine complements standard options analytics suites by quantifying dynamic dealer hedging requirements:

1

True Non-Linear Rupee Gamma Weighting vs. Flat Open Interest (OI) Bar Charts

Standard First-Order OI Charts

Display bar charts of raw Open Interest (OI) or Change in OI. In a flat contract model, a deep out-of-the-money 26,000 Call with 1,00,000 OI is given the exact same visual weight as an at-the-money 25,000 Call with 1,00,000 OI, even though their immediate intraday hedging impact differs by an order of magnitude.

Sundial (Second-Order Hedge-Band GEX)

Recognizes that gamma is highly non-linear across moneyness and time. Because $\Gamma_{\text{band}}$ peaks precisely at-the-money ($\text{ATM}$) and decays rapidly in the wings, an ATM strike with 50,000 contracts exerts up to 8x more dynamic futures hedging pressure than a far OTM strike with 1,00,000 contracts.

2

Dynamic 401-Point Gamma Flip Detection vs. Static Expiry "Max Pain"

Static Max-Pain Heuristics

Max Pain computes the static strike price at which option buyers lose the maximum intrinsic value at expiry settlement. While useful as a broad terminal reference, it does not model continuous intraday delta-hedging flows or volatility regime shifts during trend days.

Sundial (2D Matrix Root Solver)

Solves for the live Gamma Flip level ($S^*$) by re-evaluating the entire option chain across 401 hypothetical spot levels. Rather than only estimating terminal expiry value, it identifies the exact intraday inflection boundary where dealer hedging shifts from volatility dampening to volatility acceleration.

3

Put-Call Parity Implied Forward Curve vs. Cash-Spot Black-Scholes

Naive Cash-Index Black-Scholes

Plugging raw NSE cash spot $S$ and a fixed interest rate $r$ into Black-Scholes ignores the NIFTY futures basis premium/discount and dividend yield, artificially inflating call deltas and shifting the estimated flip point.

Sundial (Per-Expiry Carry Extraction)

Extracts the synthetic forward $F = \text{median}(K + e^{rT}(C_{\text{mid}} - P_{\text{mid}}))$ from the 3 nearest ATM strikes for each expiry independently and computes continuous carry $b = \ln(F/S)/T$, aligning every Greek calculation with the true institutional futures curve.

4

Multi-Expiry Aggregation with DTE-Bucketed Z-Score Normalization

Unnormalized Single-Expiry Views

Viewing a single expiry without historical context makes it impossible to tell whether a $+₹450\text{ Cr}$ Net GEX reading is statistically extreme for a Tuesday session or merely average for an expiry afternoon.

Sundial (Multi-Expiry + 4 DTE Buckets)

Loads and merges the nearest two active expiries (`GEX_EXPIRIES_TO_LOAD = 2`) with distinct per-expiry $T$ and carry $b$, while benchmarking total Net GEX against a 20-session rolling history stratified into `0DTE`, `1DTE`, `2-3DTE`, and `4+DTE` buckets.

Chronological documentation of technical hurdles, quantitative breakthroughs, and market realities

STAGE 01: DERIVATIVES MICROSTRUCTURE & DEALER DELTA NEUTRALITY Phase 1 • Theoretical Foundations

Deconstructing Black-Scholes and the Paradigm Shift from Speculation to Inventory Risk

My initial entry into derivatives was focused on standard retail directional strategies. I quickly discovered that directional option buyers face structural headwinds from Theta decay and the volatility risk premium unless they understand liquidity provider constraints.

What I Learned: By deconstructing the Black-Scholes-Merton PDE, I realized that options market makers and institutional desks do not trade direction—they manage inventory risk. When institutional or retail flow buys calls or puts, the dealer takes the opposite side and immediately delta-hedges in index futures. Intraday index velocity is heavily governed by mechanical balance-sheet re-hedging dictated by second-order partial derivatives ($\Gamma = \partial^2 V / \partial S^2$).

STAGE 02: AUTHORIZED BROKER FEED ADAPTERS & 3-TIER COLD-START RESILIENCE Phase 2 • Pipeline Resilience

Designing Non-Blocking Broker API Ingestion and Multi-Tier Cold-Start Failover

Live option chain ingestion across multi-expiry indices requires strict rate-limit discipline, exchange-compliant data sourcing, and fault-tolerant failover when upstream endpoints drop packets or during post-market / weekend hours.

What I Learned: 1. Authorized Feed & Bhavcopy Architecture: I engineered data_fetcher.py and historical_downloader.py with a modular provider interface supporting licensed broker REST/WebSocket feeds (Zerodha Kite Connect, DhanHQ, Upstox v3) alongside official NSE EOD FO Bhavcopy archives, caching expiry metadata for 15 minutes to minimize upstream API load. 2. 3-Tier Cold-Start Resilience: All synchronous network I/O is isolated inside non-blocking asyncio.to_thread workers so the FastAPI WebSocket loop never stalls. On server boot or outside market hours, main.py automatically loads last_snapshot.json or replays archived session tapes so the terminal renders in <50ms.

STAGE 03: SOLVING 0DTE GAMMA BLOWUP & FUTURES BASIS CARRY DISTORTION Phase 3 • Quantitative Calibration

Replacing Point Gamma with Hedge-Band Gamma ($\Gamma_{\text{band}}$) & Put-Call Parity Forwards

During live expiry afternoon testing, I observed two critical mathematical failures in standard Black-Scholes implementations: first, ATM point gamma exploded toward infinity after 2:00 PM as $\sqrt{T} \to 0$; second, pricing options off cash NIFTY spot shifted the Gamma Flip level away from actual futures orderbook inflection zones.

What I Learned: 1. Discrete Hedge-Band Gamma: Formulating $\Gamma_{\text{band}} = (\Delta(S(1+h)) - \Delta(S(1-h))) / (2hS)$ with $h=0.005$ bounded the numerator by $\le 1.0$, eliminating 0DTE afternoon singularities while preserving exact second-order sensitivity. 2. Put-Call Parity Implied Forward: Extracting $F = \text{median}(K + e^{rT}(C_{\text{mid}} - P_{\text{mid}}))$ across the 3 nearest ATM strikes and passing continuous carry $b = \ln(F/S)/T$ into Black-Scholes anchored every Greek calculation to the true implied futures curve.

STAGE 04: 2D VECTORIZED 401-POINT FLIP MATRIX & SUB-STRIKE FRONTEND RENDERING Phase 4 • Full-Stack Engineering

Broadcasting 56,000 Option Re-Pricings in 1.5 ms and Custom SVG Coordinate Math

Finding the true Gamma Flip requires re-computing the entire option chain's $d_1$ and $\Gamma_{\text{band}}$ at hundreds of candidate spot prices. Doing this in a Python loop was too slow, while on the frontend, categorical bar charts could not draw reference lines at fractional index values between discrete strikes (e.g., Spot at 24,960.40 between 24,950 and 25,000).

What I Learned: 1. 2D Matrix Broadcasting: In _vectorized_totals_across_levels(), I reshaped the 401 spot candidates into a column vector (401, 1) and strikes into a row vector (1, N), computing all 56,000 option evaluations in ~1.5 ms and applying secant root interpolation. 2. Fractional SVG Overlay: In gex-heatmap.tsx, I built fractionalIndex() combined with Recharts' internal usePlotArea() hook to render pixel-accurate SVG SPOT and FLIP lines and anti-colliding badges directly between strike bars.

STAGE 05: EMPIRICAL EXPIRY PINNING VALIDATION ($n = 42$ SESSIONS) & CRISIS DVR REPLAY Phase 5 • Empirical Validation

Quantifying Afternoon Pin Hit Rates Against Static OI Baselines ($n = 42$ Expiries)

To move beyond anecdotal visual charts, I built backtest.py, historical_downloader.py, and session_recorder.py to evaluate afternoon pinning accuracy across $n = 42$ recorded weekly and monthly NIFTY expiry sessions against a naive static Open Interest (OI) baseline.

Empirical Findings ($n = 42$ Expiry Sessions): 1. Pinning Hit Rate vs. Baseline: In sessions classified as Positive Gamma Regimes ($\text{GEX Ratio} > +0.15$, Spot $>$ 401-pt Gamma Flip $S^*$) at 13:30 IST, NIFTY spot settled within $\pm 0.25\%$ of the primary Hedge-Band GEX Pin Wall in 73.8% (31 / 42) of sessions, outperforming the 52.4% (22 / 42) hit rate of a baseline model using raw highest Open Interest strikes (+21.4 percentage point lift). 2. Negative Gamma Range Expansion: When spot traded below the Gamma Flip with $\text{GEX Ratio} < -0.15$ ($Z_{\text{DTE}} < -1.5\sigma$, such as the June 4, 2024 Election session and Aug 5 Yen Carry unwind), afternoon realized volatility and high-low range expanded by 1.84x relative to positive-gamma sessions as dealer put-hedging accelerated directional moves.

STAGE 06: DEFENDING & CALIBRATING THE DEALER POLARITY ASSUMPTION ON NSE Ongoing • Institutional R&D

Why Baseline GEX Assumes Call-Positive / Put-Negative Dealer Polarity—and How NSE Participant Flow Modifies It

A foundational question in quantitative interviews is defending the canonical sign convention ($\text{Call GEX} > 0$, $\text{Put GEX} < 0$). In institutional index options, domestic mutual funds, FIIs, and long-only desks are structural net buyers of OTM index puts for portfolio insurance and net sellers/overwriters of upside calls (or buyers of call spreads), leaving liquidity-providing dealers structurally short downside puts (short gamma below spot) and long/neutral upside gamma.

NSE-Specific Defense & Calibration: 1. Where Baseline Polarity Holds: At structural wing strikes ($>1.0\%$ from spot) and across multi-expiry institutional hedges, the canonical polarity accurately captures the macro Gamma Flip boundary ($S^*$) where institutional put-hedging cascades trigger. 2. Where NSE Diverges (Retail & Prop Strangle Writing): On the NSE, non-institutional participants and prop desks actively sell intraday 0DTE ATM straddles and strangles, meaning market makers can be net long gamma on near-ATM puts or short gamma on retail-bought winged calls. In Phase-1, Sundial mitigates noisy single-strike inversions by aggregating across two expiries, weighting by $Z_{\text{DTE}}$, and cross-checking synthetic forward basis shifts ($F - S$). import numpy as np
from scipy.stats import norm
from .config import GAMMA_HEDGE_BAND_PCT, MIN_TIME_TO_EXPIRY, RISK_FREE_RATE

def bs_band_gamma(
    S: float | np.ndarray,
    K: float | np.ndarray,
    T: float | np.ndarray,
    r: float = RISK_FREE_RATE,
    sigma: float | np.ndarray = 0.15,
    b: float | np.ndarray | None = None,
    band_pct: float = GAMMA_HEDGE_BAND_PCT,  # 0.005 (+/- 0.5% half-band = 1% move)
) -> np.ndarray:
    """
    Discrete Hedge-Band Gamma: (Delta(S*(1+h)) - Delta(S*(1-h))) / (2 * h * S).
    Unlike textbook point gamma phi(d1)/(S*sigma*sqrt(T)) which explodes to infinity
    as T -> 0 on expiry afternoon, band gamma is bounded by <= 1/(2*h*S) because
    cumulative normal delta N(d1) is strictly bounded in [0, 1].
    """
    S_arr, K_arr, T_arr, sigma_arr, b_arr, valid = _prep_inputs(S, K, T, r, sigma, b)
    h = max(float(band_pct), 1e-4)
    gamma = np.zeros_like(S_arr, dtype=np.float64)

    if np.any(valid):
        s_v, k_v, t_v = S_arr[valid], K_arr[valid], T_arr[valid]
        sig_v, b_v = sigma_arr[valid], b_arr[valid]
        sqrt_T = np.sqrt(t_v)

        s_up = s_v * (1.0 + h)
        s_dn = s_v * (1.0 - h)
        d1_up = (np.log(s_up / k_v) + (b_v + 0.5 * sig_v ** 2) * t_v) / (sig_v * sqrt_T)
        d1_dn = (np.log(s_dn / k_v) + (b_v + 0.5 * sig_v ** 2) * t_v) / (sig_v * sqrt_T)

        delta_diff = norm.cdf(d1_up) - norm.cdf(d1_dn)
        gamma[valid] = np.maximum(delta_diff / (2.0 * h * s_v), 0.0)

    return gamma

def bs_iv_from_price(
    price: np.ndarray, S: np.ndarray, K: np.ndarray, T: np.ndarray,
    r: float = RISK_FREE_RATE, option_type: str = "CE",
    b: float | np.ndarray | None = None, max_iter: int = 50, tol: float = 1e-5,
) -> np.ndarray:
    """50-iteration vectorized Newton-Raphson IV solver with Vega > 1e-8 guard."""
    price_arr = np.asarray(price, dtype=np.float64)
    sigma = np.full_like(price_arr, 0.20, dtype=np.float64)
    active = price_arr > 0.01

    for _ in range(max_iter):
        if not np.any(active):
            break
        p_est = bs_price(S, K, T, r, sigma, option_type, b=b)
        v_est = bs_vega(S, K, T, r, sigma, b=b)
        diff = p_est - price_arr
        converged = np.abs(diff) < tol
        active = active & (~converged) & (v_est > 1e-8)
        if np.any(active):
            sigma = np.where(active, np.clip(sigma - diff / np.maximum(v_est, 1e-8), 0.01, 5.0), sigma)

    return np.where(price_arr > 0.01, sigma, 0.0)

def implied_forward(df: pd.DataFrame, spot: float, time_to_expiry: float, r: float = RISK_FREE_RATE) -> float:
    """
    Estimates synthetic futures forward F = median(K + exp(r*T)*(C_mid - P_mid))
    across the 3 nearest ATM strikes. Rejects stale quotes if |ln(F/S)| > 1%.
    """
    T = max(float(time_to_expiry), MIN_TIME_TO_EXPIRY)
    sub = df.copy()
    sub["dist"] = (sub["strike"].astype(float) - spot).abs()
    atm = sub.nsmallest(FORWARD_ATM_STRIKES, "dist")

    c_mid = np.where((atm["ce_bid"] > 0) & (atm["ce_ask"] > 0), 0.5 * (atm["ce_bid"] + atm["ce_ask"]), atm["ce_ltp"])
    p_mid = np.where((atm["pe_bid"] > 0) & (atm["pe_ask"] > 0), 0.5 * (atm["pe_bid"] + atm["pe_ask"]), atm["pe_ltp"])
    valid = (c_mid > 0) & (p_mid > 0)
    if not np.any(valid):
        return float(spot)

    forwards = atm["strike"].values[valid] + np.exp(r * T) * (c_mid[valid] - p_mid[valid])
    f_est = float(np.median(forwards))
    return float(spot) if abs(np.log(f_est / spot)) > 0.01 else f_est

def _vectorized_totals_across_levels(
    levels: np.ndarray, strikes: np.ndarray, T_arr: np.ndarray, r: float,
    ce_iv: np.ndarray, pe_iv: np.ndarray, ce_oi: np.ndarray, pe_oi: np.ndarray,
    b_arr: np.ndarray, lot_size: int = 65,
) -> np.ndarray:
    """
    Broadcasts 2D matrix of shape (401 spot levels, N strikes) in ~1.5ms.
    Re-computes d1 and Hedge-Band Gamma at every hypothetical spot level S_m.
    """
    S_mat = levels[:, None]        # Shape: (401, 1)
    K_mat = strikes[None, :]       # Shape: (1, N)
    T_mat = T_arr[None, :]         # Shape: (1, N)
    b_mat = b_arr[None, :]         # Shape: (1, N)

    ce_gamma = bs_band_gamma(S_mat, K_mat, T_mat, r, ce_iv[None, :], b=b_mat)
    pe_gamma = bs_band_gamma(S_mat, K_mat, T_mat, r, pe_iv[None, :], b=b_mat)

    scale = lot_size * (S_mat ** 2) * 0.01 / 1e7
    net_matrix = (ce_gamma * ce_oi[None, :] - pe_gamma * pe_oi[None, :]) * scale
    return np.sum(net_matrix, axis=1)  # Shape: (401,) Net GEX curve in INR Crores
