PHASE 01 • 2019 (AGE 13) — THE MAIDEN TRADE & PENNY STOCK CRUCIBLE Father's Demat Account

The Urja Global Experiment: When a ₹1.20 Share Taught Me the Reality of Penny Stocks

In 2019, at age 13, I placed my very first stock market trade using my father’s demat account. I bought 250 shares of Urja Global Limited at ₹1.20 per share (a total capital outlay of ~₹300).

Shortly after buying, I watched the stock plummet by almost half its value down to the ₹0.60–₹0.70 range. I experienced the psychological sting of holding a steep unrealized paper drawdown. However, I held through the decline. Eventually, an operator-fueled microcap rally pushed the stock up to ₹2.50 per share, where I liquidated all 250 shares, booking a >100% gain on paper.

The Formative Lesson & First Rule of Capital:

While the trade ended with a monetary profit, it delivered my single most crucial early investing breakthrough: I realized I had been completely naive in assuming that a low nominal share price meant a stock was "cheap."

I recognized that microcaps and penny stocks are overwhelmingly operated vehicles for pump-and-dump schemes. If a company is valued at a ₹100 Crore market capitalization, the market has priced it there for a reason. That trade led to my first inviolable investing rule: Never chase penny stocks, and never mistake low nominal share price for intrinsic business valuation.

PHASE 02 • LATE 2019 TO EARLY 2020 (AGE 13–14) — THE 6-MONTH INTENSIVE & THE CRYPTO INQUIRY Foundational Self-Education

Building Mental Models: Coffee Can Investing, Earnings Reality & A Tuition Teacher's Bitcoin Warning

Having discarded penny stock speculation, I spent the subsequent six months systematically studying the foundational concepts of financial markets. For a 13-to-14-year-old, this meant grounding myself in core economic realities:

  • Asset Class Disaggregation: Learning the structural behaviors and risk-return profiles of equities, fixed-income debt, cash, and commodities.
  • The Earnings Anchor: Understanding dividend yields and discovering that long-term equity returns are fundamentally tethered to underlying business earnings growth ($P = EPS \times P/E$).
  • Coffee Can Compounding & Buffett Maxims: Absorbing Saurabh Mukherjea's Coffee Can Investing philosophy (allocating patient capital into high-ROCE, clean-governance market leaders) and studying Warren Buffett's timeless shareholder letters.
The Early Bitcoin Inquiry (Age 13–14):

During this period, I also began studying cryptocurrency and Bitcoin in an analytical, nuanced way well before the retail speculative wave. Fascinated by decentralized ledger mechanics, I approached my tuition teacher to ask whether we should allocate capital to Bitcoin.

"There is no regulatory guidance here. We cannot and should not invest in this criminal money."

His reaction reflected the prevailing traditional skepticism toward decentralized digital assets in India. Rather than deterring me, it sparked a lasting curiosity regarding sovereign regulatory frameworks, monetary history, and institutional balance-sheet adoption—which years later led to my thematic research on Bitcoin Corporate Treasuries.

PHASE 03 • 2020 TO 2021 (AGE 14–15) — THE MARCH 2020 CRASH & THE SYSTEMATIC SIP Mother's Account • Verified Holdings Audit

March 2020 Baptism by Fire: Turning 50% Index Drawdowns into Systematic Discipline

In March 2020, I witnessed my first major market crash: global circuit breakers, widespread panic selling, and benchmark indices plummeting by 40% to 50% in weeks. Because I had just spent months studying market history, I recognized that severe drawdowns are recurring, normal phenomena and represent the single best generational buying windows.

Naturally, my parents were initially hesitant to trust a 14-year-old with household savings during an unprecedented macroeconomic crisis. However, through persistent reasoning, I convinced them to initiate a systematic investment plan (SIP) of ₹2,000 per month using my mother’s account, which we gradually scaled to ₹6,000 per month as conviction deepened.

PHASE 04 • 2021 TO 2023 (AGE 16–17) — CLASS 11TH & 12TH: SYSTEMATIC STUDY OF F&O, IPOS & INVESTMENT INSTRUMENTS Class 11th–12th Core Learning Milestone

Broadening the Horizon: Complete Grounding in Futures & Options, Primary Market IPOs & Multi-Asset Instruments

During Class 11th and 12th, while maintaining academic rigor, I deliberately expanded my focus from simple individual equity stock-picking to mastering the full architecture of capital markets. I dedicated these two formative years to systematically studying three foundational pillars: Futures & Options (F&O), Initial Public Offerings (IPOs), and the entire spectrum of Diverse Investment Instruments across the Indian and global capital landscapes.

1. Futures & Options (F&O)

Deconstructed derivatives mathematics and options market plumbing:

  • Contract Mechanics: Cost-of-carry futures pricing, basis, Open Interest (OI) buildup, and Put-Call Ratio (PCR) sentiment analysis.
  • Option Greeks: Mathematical pricing sensitivity—Delta ($\Delta$), Gamma ($\Gamma$), Theta ($\Theta$ time decay), Vega ($\mathcal{V}$ volatility), and IV skews.
  • Risk-Defined Strategies: Iron condors, vertical spreads, calendar spreads, and non-directional hedging architectures.
Capital Reality Revelation: Discovered that systematic option selling requires deep institutional margin buffers to survive tail-risk expansions, shifting my focus to market-maker hedging dynamics and eventually leading to my proprietary GEX quantitative engine.
2. IPOs & Primary Markets

Dissected primary market capital formation and forensic prospectus diligence:

  • DRHP & Prospectus Audit: Scrutinizing Draft Red Herring Prospectuses (DRHP) for object of the issue, promoter litigation, and related-party dealings.
  • OFS vs. Fresh Issue: Evaluating whether proceeds fund real business CAPEX vs. early venture/promoter liquidity exits.
  • Book Building Mechanics: Anchor investor bidding books, QIB/NII/Retail quota subscriptions, and GMP (Grey Market Premium) vs. post-listing fair value.
Listing Lesson: Realized that listing-day hype frequently misprices equity; patient long-term investors wait for lock-in expirations and post-listing quarterly earnings confirmations.
3. Diverse Asset Instruments

Mapped the full hierarchy of financial instruments to master capital allocation:

  • Direct Equities & ETFs: Large-cap vs microcap liquidity, passive index replication, and factor/thematic ETFs.
  • Fixed Income & Debt: Government Securities (G-Secs), T-Bills, corporate bonds, yield-to-maturity (YTM), and duration risk.
  • Alternative Yield Vehicles: Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs), Infrastructure Investment Trusts (InvITs), and Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs).
Holistic Perspective: Learned how capital migrates between sovereign yield curves, credit spreads, equity risk premiums, and alternative assets across interest rate cycles.
The Class 11th–12th Synthesis:

Mastering F&O, IPO primary listings, and diverse investment vehicles during my high school years transformed how I view financial markets. Instead of viewing stocks in isolation, I began evaluating companies within the entire macroeconomic and market microstructure matrix—paving the way for my institutional equity research reports and quantitative derivatives models in university.
